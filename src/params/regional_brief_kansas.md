# Regional brief — field identity

Reference DATA for the analyst. Facts and citations only: nothing here directs an
action, a method, or an interval — reading the rocks stays your job.

## Where these wells are

- The wells in this batch belong to the Schaben field area, Ness County, Kansas
  (well headers carry PLSS section/township/range locations). That is the extent of
  the geological background provided: what the rocks are, where the reservoir sits,
  and what the water does are for the DATA to say, not this brief.

## Log-era caveats (facts of the acquisition era, not of this field)

- Wells logged before the mid-1970s commonly carry a count-rate neutron record
  (CPS/API units, single detector) instead of a compensated porosity reading; such
  records need a transform before any porosity reading, and that transform degrades
  precision (SPWLA literature on legacy neutron logs; general).
- Natural gamma in carbonate rocks can respond to uranium associated with fractures
  or stylolites rather than clay; a hot gamma streak in a carbonate is not
  automatically shale (SPWLA/textbook fact, general).
- Old paper-digitized records may carry depth-shift and unit inconsistencies; header
  units deserve skepticism (era fact, general).
